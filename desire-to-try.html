AI, HUMAN EXPERIENCE, AND THE DESIRE TO TRY

The danger is not only that AI may replace work. The danger is that the belief in near-term replacement can destroy the reason to begin, practice, struggle, master, discover and create before the replacement even arrives.
Faultline Financial · September 2026
THE CAPABILITY DOES NOT HAVE TO DISAPPEAR FIRST.
THE DESIRE WILL DISAPPEAR FIRST.

Context: Elon Musk's G20 argument

This argument was prompted in part by Elon Musk's remarks at the G20 Innovation Ministerial in North Carolina on September 1, 2026. Musk presented a highly optimistic economic case for rapid AI deployment while also making unusually short-horizon predictions about human competitiveness in digital work.

Musk estimated that digital AI could increase the global economy by roughly 20% to 30%, or on the order of $20 trillion to $30 trillion per year. He also predicted that AI would be able to do essentially anything digital that does not require physically shaping atoms by the end of 2027.

His software prediction is especially important to the argument on this page. Musk compared future AI programming capability to Stockfish, the chess engine that can defeat the strongest human chess players. He predicted that sometime next year AI software would become “Stockfish-level” good, meaning that, in his view, a human would no longer be able to compete with AI at writing software. He extended the same short horizon more broadly, saying AI would become extremely capable across engineering and digital work within roughly 12 to 18 months.

Musk paired this with an even larger robotics forecast: he argued that humanoid robots could eventually multiply global economic output many times over, and he predicted more than a billion humanoid robots within about a decade. His overall case was one of extraordinary abundance, productivity, and economic expansion.

This page asks what that economic argument leaves out. If people hear that the skill they are beginning to learn may become noncompetitive within 12 to 18 months, the effect is not confined to a future labor market. The prediction can change motivation now. A person can stop studying, practicing, painting, programming, researching, or trying before the predicted capability even arrives.

The contrast is fundamental: Musk's argument measures AI primarily through output, productivity, abundance, and economic growth. The argument here focuses on what those measurements do not capture: process, mastery, participation, curiosity, discovery, motivation, and the human desire to try.

The central issue

The central concern is not simply whether AI can produce better outputs. The concern is what happens to the desire to learn, practice, struggle, discover, create, and master something when people are told that within 12–18 months a machine will be vastly better at the very activity they are trying to learn.

A distant prediction leaves room for a lifetime of mastery. A 12–18 month prediction says, in effect: why even start? The prediction can change behavior in the present before anyone knows whether the prediction is true.

AI does not have to replace a person first. The belief that it soon will can extinguish the desire to try before the promised technology arrives.

The process is the point

The finished output is not the whole experience.

Painting is not merely possessing a picture. Coding is not merely obtaining software. Mathematics is not merely obtaining an answer. Physics is not merely receiving a theory. Chess is not merely determining a winner. Research is not merely obtaining findings.

The value lives in observing, attempting, failing, correcting, learning, developing judgment, noticing something unexpected, and finally understanding or creating something through one's own effort.

DOING IT IS THE BENEFIT.
MASTERY IS THE GOAL.
THE EFFORT IS WHERE THE EXPERIENCE LIVES.

Chess

The Stockfish analogy is not abstract. Once computers became so much better at chess that the challenge felt trivialized, the enjoyment disappeared. The loss was not merely the pleasure of winning. The entire process lost its meaning: learning, experimenting, anticipating, failing, improving, discovering, and finally getting something right.

Coding

Learning Java, BASIC, Perl, Python, and HTML was not merely a way to obtain finished software. The value was learning how to make it, solving problems, understanding why something failed, and developing mastery. Supervising AI is a different activity from becoming capable of building the thing oneself.

Art

The value of painting and drawing was not merely possessing a finished image. It was observing, choosing, struggling, experimenting, developing technique, creating, and eventually producing something through one's own ability.

Math, physics, science, and research

The value is in figuring something out: confronting a problem, thinking, experimenting, failing, revising an idea, and finally understanding something that was not understood before.

If AI eliminates trial and error, it eliminates a fundamental way we discover.

THE SCIENTIFIC METHOD DOES NOT DISAPPEAR. THE SCIENTIST DISAPPEARS FROM THE LOOP.

The prediction becomes real before the technology does

A near-term prediction does not have to come true to change what people do now.

If someone believes the skill they are beginning to learn will become economically or intellectually pointless within a year, that belief changes whether they enroll, practice, persist, experiment, or devote years of their life to becoming good at it.

The future prediction becomes causally real before it becomes factually verified.

A lifespan cannot be repriced backward. The years a person decides not to spend learning something because they were told there would soon be no point cannot simply be restored later if the forecast was wrong.

TWELVE TO EIGHTEEN MONTHS DOES NOT ONLY DESCRIBE THE FUTURE.
IT REPRICES THE VALUE OF TRYING TODAY.

Innovation depends on people wanting to begin

Innovation does not begin with an output. It begins with curiosity and the desire to try. People wonder, experiment, fail, notice anomalies, pursue strange ideas, learn from mistakes, and continue without a guarantee of success.

Efficiency asks how to make today's thing faster or cheaper. Innovation asks what might exist that nobody has thought of yet.

THE DESIRE TO TRY IS AN INPUT INTO INNOVATION.

Companies are consuming the accumulated products of human curiosity while simultaneously building systems that can weaken the reason future people have to acquire the same depth of knowledge.

If people stop entering difficult fields, stop becoming experts, stop learning the underlying mechanics, and stop spending years struggling with unanswered questions, the system does not merely automate work. It begins consuming the human source from which new work, new questions, anomalies, discoveries, and breakthroughs arise.

Work, income, and participation

If AI genuinely performs everything a worker provides to a company faster, cheaper, and at higher quality, the economic reason to employ that worker disappears.

If labor stops being the primary way people earn a claim on society's output, something else will have to replace the paycheck.

But income is only one problem.

Giving people enough money to consume does not restore the experience of creating, discovering, mastering, contributing, or being needed.

THERE ARE TWO AI PROBLEMS: DISTRIBUTION AND PARTICIPATION.

Aggregate abundance does not answer who receives the gains. And even perfect distribution would not answer what achievement means when human capability is no longer required to accomplish most things.

LEISURE IS NOT A SUBSTITUTE FOR BEING NEEDED.
CONSUMPTION IS NOT A SUBSTITUTE FOR PARTICIPATION.
ABUNDANCE IS NOT A SUBSTITUTE FOR ACHIEVEMENT.

Output versus human experience

Technology is supposed to be instrumental. Flourishing is supposed to be the objective.

That distinction disappears when every human activity is evaluated only by whether a machine can produce the same measurable output faster.

There is no conventional economic statistic for “a person stopped painting,” “a person stopped studying physics,” “a person stopped learning to program,” or “a person no longer wants to discover something.”

Those losses can occur while measured productivity rises.

“AI can do it for you” answers the wrong question when the purpose was never merely to obtain the output. The purpose was to experience doing it.

Bottom line

AI does not have to eliminate every human capability to change civilization.

It can change civilization earlier by changing what people believe is worth becoming good at.

That is the deeper danger in short-horizon claims of human obsolescence.

The machine does not have to take the skill first.

It only has to convince the human that learning the skill is pointless.

THE LOSS IS NOT ONLY THE JOB.
THE LOSS IS THE REASON TO TRY.

AND IF THE DESIRE TO TRY IS WHERE DISCOVERY BEGINS, THAT LOSS REACHES FAR BEYOND EMPLOYMENT.
