This has happened before

Financial history is full of moments when authorities stepped in to stop market stress from cascading through the system.

1987 — Black Monday

After the crash, the Federal Reserve pledged liquidity support to the financial system. Market functioning was stabilized. The intervention became a precedent for using liquidity to stop financial panic from feeding on itself.

1998 — Long-Term Capital Management

The Federal Reserve facilitated a private rescue that prevented LTCM's leveraged positions from being dumped into already fragile markets. The fire sale was interrupted before the underlying leverage problem had somehow ceased to exist.

2008 — The Global Financial Crisis

Emergency lending facilities, near-zero rates and large-scale asset purchases were deployed as the financial system broke. The market received enormous support. The economy still fell into the Great Recession.

2020 — The Pandemic Crisis

The Federal Reserve bought Treasuries, mortgage securities, corporate bonds and bond ETFs while opening emergency facilities. Market functioning recovered much faster than the physical economy. The screen healed before the world did.

The lesson is not that intervention never works.

The lesson is that intervention can silence the market's warning before it repairs the conditions that produced the warning.

That difference is everything.
