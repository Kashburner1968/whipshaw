How the Machine Turns Fed Words Into Market Code

A Federal Reserve governor can speak in paragraphs. The market machine can reduce those paragraphs to variables, probabilities and directional signals before the physical economy has changed at all.

Faultline Financial · September 3, 2026

Christopher Waller did not lower an interest rate when he spoke Thursday. He did not lower oil. He did not lower the 30-year Treasury yield by decree. He did not change a mortgage payment, a corporate refinancing bill or a household budget.

He changed words.

That was enough to change what machines could calculate.

THE HUMAN HEARS A SPEECH.

THE MACHINE HEARS FEATURES, WEIGHTS, PROBABILITIES AND DELTAS.

That distinction matters because modern financial NLP systems can turn central-bank language into structured inputs fast enough to affect the market before the economic conditions being discussed have materially changed.

Waller's September 3 speech supplied an unusually clean example. He said inflation remained meaningfully above the Federal Reserve's 2% goal. He also said recent data showed signs of disinflation and that, if the improvement continued, he would be inclined to support holding the federal funds rate steady. He simultaneously warned that hotter August inflation could justify a hike.

To a human reader, that is a conditional argument.

To a machine, it can become a change in the distribution of possible outcomes.

1. The speech becomes data

The first transformation is brutally simple. Text is ingested, cleaned and broken into tokens or token groups. The machine does not need to admire the sentence. It needs to represent it mathematically.

SPEECH → TEXT INGESTION → TOKENIZATION → PHRASE / ENTITY DETECTION → FINANCIAL CONTEXT

Names such as Waller and Federal Reserve, concepts such as inflation, energy prices, policy rate, hold and rate hike, and phrases such as signs of disinflation can become machine-readable features.

The sentence has begun its conversion into code.

2. Finance has its own language

Ordinary sentiment analysis is not enough. Financial language is specialized. That is why finance-specific resources such as the Loughran-McDonald sentiment dictionaries and models such as FinBERT exist.

The Loughran-McDonald framework classifies financial language into categories including negative, positive, uncertainty, strong and weak modal language, and constraining language. FinBERT takes the idea further by using a language model trained or adapted specifically for financial text.

A FINANCIAL NLP ENGINE IS NOT ASKING WHETHER A SENTENCE SOUNDS HAPPY.

It is asking what the language implies for rates, inflation, growth, risk, earnings, volatility and asset prices.

That is why the same word can carry a different meaning depending on context. The market meaning of tightening is not merely linguistic negativity. It is a potential change in the price of money.

3. The machine is looking for the change

This is where the process becomes especially important for Waller's speech.

The market does not have to ask only, “Is this speech hawkish or dovish?” A quantitative system can compare the new communication with prior language, expectations and policy probabilities.

It can ask a more powerful question:

WHAT CHANGED?

Waller's absolute message still contained inflation risk. But his forward-looking language contained a less-hawkish branch: recent data were showing signs of disinflation and continued improvement could make him support holding rates steady.

The machine can trade that delta.

PRIOR POLICY LANGUAGE / EXPECTATIONS ↓ NEW WALLER LANGUAGE ↓ CONTEXTUAL COMPARISON ↓ HAWKISH / DOVISH SHIFT ↓ UPDATED RATE EXPECTATION

This is the same divide Faultline Financial has been tracking in the bond market:

THE MARKET TRADES THE CHANGE. THE ECONOMY LIVES WITH THE LEVEL.

Inflation can still be above target. Energy can still be expensive. The 30-year yield can still be above 5.2%. But if the incoming language moves a few degrees toward a pause, the machine has received new information.

4. A useful illustration of the machine's math

A real proprietary trading system's weights and thresholds are not public, and different models will score the same speech differently. But the mechanism can be illustrated without pretending we know any firm's private code.

Speech featureIllustrative directional weight
Inflation meaningfully above 2%-0.40 · hawkish
Energy prices remain significantly higher-0.30 · hawkish
Signs of disinflation+0.65 · dovish
Inclined to support holding if improvement continues+0.80 · dovish
Illustrative aggregate+0.25 · net relief

These numbers are an explanatory example, not observed output from a specific trading firm's model.

The point is not the exact number.

The point is what happens when a machine converts conflicting language into a single usable direction.

THE ABSOLUTE CONDITIONS CAN REMAIN BAD WHILE THE ALGEBRAIC CHANGE TURNS POSITIVE.

5. Waller explained why his words can move markets

Waller himself described central-bank communication in terms that make the mechanism unusually clear. He said he tries to communicate his reaction function: if the data arrive one way, he will advocate one policy path; if they arrive another way, he will advocate another.

He also pointed to the power of communication before an actual policy move. Discussing 2021–22, Waller said communicating that rate hikes were coming helped market interest rates rise before the Federal Reserve had actually lifted the policy rate from the effective lower bound.

That is the bridge between language and markets.

POLICY LANGUAGE → EXPECTED REACTION FUNCTION → RATE-PATH PROBABILITY → TREASURY PRICING → YIELD → CROSS-ASSET SIGNAL

The physical policy action can come later.

The expectation moves first.

6. Then the NLP signal meets the trading stack

Financial NLP does not exist in isolation. Once a speech, headline or transcript produces a machine-readable signal, that information can meet other systematic inputs: Treasury prices, yield changes, futures, volatility, options positioning, momentum and cross-asset correlations.

WALLER LANGUAGE ↓ FINANCIAL NLP / SENTIMENT / POLICY-SHIFT CLASSIFICATION ↓ RATE-EXPECTATION UPDATE ↓ TREASURY PRICE RESPONSE ↓ YIELD RESPONSE ↓ QUANT CROSS-ASSET FILTERS ↓ ES / SPY / OPTIONS / VOLATILITY RESPONSE

This is where a few words can become order flow.

Not because every trading firm uses the same model.

Not because one universal sentiment score controls Wall Street.

Because machines can translate language into variables, and variables can enter automated trading systems at machine speed.

7. The dangerous asymmetry

The speech can be parsed in milliseconds.

The Treasury market can move almost immediately.

Equity futures can respond almost immediately.

Options and volatility systems can respond to the new price state.

But none of that makes oil cheaper.

None of it makes inflation disappear.

None of it lowers a household's existing borrowing cost.

None of it repairs the physical economy in the same instant.

THE SENTENCE MOVES FIRST.

THE MONEY MOVES SECOND.

THE PHYSICAL WORLD MOVES THIRD.

THE HUMAN PAYS LAST.

8. This is how “best of the bad news” becomes machine-readable

Waller's speech contained both sides of the inflation story. Inflation remained above target. Energy remained elevated. A hike remained possible.

But the new information also contained a relief branch.

Signs of disinflation.

Inclined to support holding.

A direction-sensitive market does not require the whole problem to disappear. It requires the probability distribution to move.

THE BAD NEWS DOES NOT HAVE TO BECOME GOOD.

IT ONLY HAS TO BECOME FRACTIONALLY LESS BAD.

That is how language becomes a signal.

That is how the signal becomes a rate expectation.

That is how the rate expectation can become a bond move.

And that is how a bond move can become an equity-market input before the world underneath the market has changed.
