SPY KEEPS REFUSING TO TEST SUPPORT. THE RISK KEEPS BUILDING.

Faultline Financial · September 4, 2026

At the writing of this article, the 30-year Treasury yield has risen to 5.24%. Crude oil remains around $90 a barrel. Diesel now costs approximately $5.85 a gallon nationally.

Yet SPY has not even meaningfully tested its first support level of 769.18.

THE STOCK MARKET IS TRADING AROUND EVERY BUILT-IN WARNING SYSTEM THE ECONOMY HAS.

The bond market is warning it. The energy market is warning it. Diesel is warning it. The increased risk of another rate hike is warning it.

And SPY keeps refusing to correct.

After testing an early-morning low right down at $769.00 at 10:55 AM, the index did not break. Instead, execution bots swept the floor, forcing a steady consolidation higher into the afternoon, with SPY hovering closer to $769.60.

This would be concerning if it happened once.

It keeps happening.

Day after day, SPY is failing to meaningfully test the support levels underneath it while economic risks continue to accumulate. Corrections begin, technical levels break, and then the selling stops before the market is forced to find out where its real support actually exists.

Yesterday gave us another example of how this happens.

Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller said that recent data showed signs of disinflation and indicated that, if the improvement continued, he would support holding rates steady at the September meeting. Markets immediately responded. Rate-hike expectations fell, bond yields eased and equities rallied.

SPY closed yesterday at $773.17.

The market heard relief.

Then this morning the economic picture changed again.

The August jobs report came in at 162,000 jobs, almost three times the 56,000 economists had expected. Rate futures responded by pushing the probability of a September rate hike higher. Treasury yields moved higher with it.

The risk went back up.

SPY DIDN'T GO BACK DOWN.

It remains considerably above the price it held before Waller's remarks, even though the risk of tighter monetary policy has increased again.

And monetary policy isn't the only warning.

Energy remains expensive. Diesel is sitting at extraordinary levels. Long-term borrowing costs remain elevated. These are not warnings coming from the same place. That is what makes them important.

The Treasury market is telling us money is expensive. The energy market is telling us operating the physical economy is expensive. Diesel is transmitting that energy shock directly into transportation, agriculture and distribution. The jobs report is telling the Federal Reserve that the labor market may be strong enough to tolerate another rate hike.

The market has received warning after warning.

But SPY keeps trading around them.

THE PIVOT BROKE. SPY STILL WOULD NOT CORRECT.

When SPY crossed cleanly below its critical intraday pivot point of 770.11, the move should have opened the door to the support sitting underneath it.

It didn't.

SPY broke 770.11 while the economic pressure was already there. Treasury yields were rising. Crude oil remained expensive. Diesel was extraordinarily expensive. The probability of another rate hike had increased.

But the selling did not accelerate.

SPY reached $769.00. S1 was sitting at 769.18. SPY moved through it intraday, but there was no meaningful downside follow-through. There was no sustained break through support that forced the market into a deeper repricing.

Instead, SPY stabilized. The market consolidated. Then it began moving higher again.

THE PIVOT BROKE. THE MACRO RISK WAS THERE. THE FOLLOW-THROUGH WASN'T.

That matters because there were several mechanisms capable of turning the loss of the pivot into a deeper correction.

1. LONG-TRIGGERED STOP LIQUIDATIONS

The moment 770.11 broke, the algorithms protecting the previous day's Waller-induced rally should have recognized a trend reversal.

Institutional stop-loss and sell-stop orders clustered beneath the 770.11 pivot should have begun executing. That selling should have added pressure to the break, opening the path through S1 at 769.18 and toward the next layer of support.

But it didn't.

SPY touched $769.00, barely moving beneath S1, and stopped. The selling never developed into a sustained break of support. Instead, SPY stabilized and began consolidating higher.

2. CROSS-ASSET RISK OFFLOADS

SPY was not breaking 770.11 in isolation.

Long bonds were selling off. The 30-year Treasury yield was rising. Diesel was extraordinarily expensive. Crude oil remained elevated. The probability of another rate hike had increased.

Those signals were coming from different markets, but they were pointing toward the same problem:

THE COST OF MONEY AND THE COST OF OPERATING THE PHYSICAL ECONOMY WERE BOTH MOVING HIGHER.

Multi-asset algorithms should have recognized that convergence. Equity weakness below 770.11, combined with higher yields and rising energy costs, should have triggered broader risk reduction.

It didn't.

The bond market was repricing. The rate market was repricing. The physical economy was carrying the cost of expensive fuel.

SPY barely moved.

3. MOMENTUM-BOT SHORT INVERSION

Then there are the machines built specifically to trade momentum.

Once SPY crossed 770.11, short-term momentum models should have recognized the loss of the pivot as a downside signal. Passive buying should have weakened. Selling pressure should have increased. Short-side execution should have begun pressing the move through support.

Instead, SPY reached $769.00 and stopped. The breakdown did not expand. The market consolidated. Then it began moving higher again.

770.11 BROKE. THE MACRO RISK WAS THERE. THE FOLLOW-THROUGH WASN'T.

One technical level does not guarantee a correction. That isn't the point.

The point is that this keeps happening.

Day after day, SPY approaches or crosses an intraday level that should expose the next layer of support. The economic risks have not disappeared. The long end of the Treasury market remains under pressure. Energy remains expensive. Diesel remains extraordinarily expensive. Rate-hike risk has increased.

Yet the deeper correction repeatedly fails to develop.

And yesterday's reaction to Waller makes that asymmetry impossible to ignore.

When Waller gave the market language it interpreted as relief, the machines responded immediately. Rate-hike expectations dropped. Yields responded. Equities rallied. SPY closed at $773.17.

Then the jobs report arrived.

162,000 JOBS.

The expectation was 56,000. Rate-hike probability jumped again. Treasury yields jumped again. The economic warning returned.

But the equity market did not surrender yesterday's move with anything approaching the same urgency.

THE MARKET PRICED THE RELIEF IMMEDIATELY.

IT HAS NOT PRICED THE RETURN OF THE RISK THE SAME WAY.

That asymmetry is the story.

Because the index-tracking algorithms are heavily weighted toward macro policy signals—like Governor Waller's remarks—the algorithmic buyers treated the breach below 770.11 as noise rather than a structural warning. The words coming from the Federal Reserve carried more weight than the rising cost of energy, the rising 30-year Treasury yield, and the increased risk of another rate hike.

SPY fell to $769.00 and stopped. It briefly crossed S1 at 769.18, but never developed the sustained break through support that would have forced a deeper correction.

The market had every opportunity to continue.

It didn't.

The algorithms bought the weakness instead.

By short-circuiting what should have been a routine, healthy technical correction down through support, the algorithms have swept the real economic data under the rug. Each interrupted correction leaves more risk sitting underneath a market that refuses to price it.

And because this keeps happening, the problem compounds.

THE CORRECTION DOESN'T DISAPPEAR. THE RISK THAT SHOULD HAVE BEEN PRICED INTO IT REMAINS.

The market isn't eliminating the risk.

IT IS POSTPONING THE PRICE OF IT.
