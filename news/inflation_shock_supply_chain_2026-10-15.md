# THE INFLATION SHOCK IS MOVING THROUGH THE SUPPLY CHAIN

**Whipshaw | October 15, 2026 | Economic documentation**

The September inflation releases show pressure at both ends of the economy. The Consumer Price Index rose **3.7% over twelve months** and **0.6% in September**. Producer prices rose **6.3% over twelve months** and **1.3% in September**, the largest monthly increase since March 2022.

The gasoline shock is already in consumers' bills: **CPI gasoline +9.1% in September, +34.2% over twelve months**. The Bureau of Labor Statistics says gasoline accounted for more than half of September's monthly all-items CPI increase.

The upstream pipeline is under pressure too: the PPI report records **producer gasoline +20.3% and diesel +21.0% in September**. Prices for final-demand transportation and warehousing services increased **3.4%**. Rising freight, fuel and input prices can squeeze margins or be passed through to customers, with timing and magnitude varying across industries.

**Headline retail sales are not a household-welfare index.** September retail sales rose **0.8%**, but sales excluding gasoline stations rose only **0.2%** and the retail control group just **0.1%**. Higher gasoline spending can lift nominal retail receipts while reducing what households can afford elsewhere.

The annual PPI and CPI figures are not directly interchangeable: they cover different baskets and stages of production. The difference between them does not by itself predict future CPI. Their simultaneous acceleration nevertheless documents substantial pressure on producers and consumers.

**Whipshaw's warning:** investors, policymakers and households should not mistake gasoline-driven nominal spending for broad-based real demand. Rising input prices, elevated consumer inflation and a weak underlying retail-sales control group belong in the same economic account.
