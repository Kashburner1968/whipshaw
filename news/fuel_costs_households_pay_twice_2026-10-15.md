# WHEN FUEL COSTS RISE, HOUSEHOLDS PAY TWICE

**Whipshaw | October 15, 2026 | Economic impact briefing**

The September retail-sales headline was **+0.8%**. Yet sales excluding gasoline stations increased only **0.2%**, and the narrower control group rose just **0.1%**. Gasoline-station sales increased **6.4%**. These nominal figures measure money spent, not additional goods purchased or improvements in purchasing power.

The same day, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported a **1.3% monthly increase in September producer prices**. Energy-intensive transport and production can pass higher costs along the supply chain, compress business margins, or both. The exact pass-through varies by contract, sector, inventory and competitive conditions.

## The household squeeze

**First payment: at the pump.** Rising gasoline prices can force households to devote more of their budgets to transportation without increasing miles driven. A nominal retail-sales increase driven by gasoline is not necessarily a sign of stronger real consumption.

**Second payment: through the supply chain.** Diesel and energy are inputs to freight, agricultural production, manufacturing and distribution. Businesses may absorb some costs through lower margins or pass some through to consumers. This is a transmission mechanism, not a claim that every product price has already risen by the same amount.

**Third pressure: weaker discretionary demand.** When necessities take a larger share of household spending, other purchases can be delayed or reduced. The September control-group figure offers a useful counterweight to the stronger headline.

## Why the equity-market narrative matters

On October 12, BlackRock Investment Institute moved its tactical U.S. equities view from overweight to neutral and upgraded short-term U.S. Treasuries to overweight. That research allocation change belongs in the same dated public record as retail-sales composition, producer inflation and observed SPY price movements. It is not a disclosed measure of executed sales.

**Whipshaw's warning:** headline nominal growth can coexist with deteriorating affordability. A market commentary that celebrates the top-line retail figure without explaining the gasoline contribution omits information essential to understanding household conditions.
