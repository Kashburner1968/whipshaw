# THE MARKET IS PRICING OPTIMISM. THE ECONOMY IS REPORTING STRESS.

**Whipshaw Market Documentation | October 15, 2026**

The September retail-sales headline rose **0.8%**, beating forecasts, but the underlying figures tell a materially different story. Sales excluding gasoline stations rose only **0.2%**; the retail-sales control group rose just **0.1%**, down from **1.2%** in August. Gasoline-station sales climbed **6.4%**. The headline measures dollars spent, not inflation-adjusted purchasing power. More money changing hands at the pump is not the same thing as households buying more goods.

At the same time, September producer prices rose **1.3%** month over month, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics. RSM's October 15 analysis described weakening underlying demand and rising input-cost pressure. This combination threatens household budgets and business margins even as the headline sales figure appears strong.

Institutional research is also becoming more cautious. In its October 12 weekly commentary, the **BlackRock Investment Institute moved U.S. equities from overweight to neutral**, upgraded short-term U.S. Treasuries to overweight, and shifted longer-term Treasuries to neutral. BlackRock continues to favor AI-related investments within U.S. equities; its shift is a tactical research allocation view, not a disclosed sale amount.

**Whipshaw's thesis:** a strong headline and a rising equity index can coexist with worsening conditions in the physical economy. The meaningful question is not simply whether nominal spending rose, but how much of that spending represents higher prices rather than more real consumption, and who absorbs the resulting costs.
