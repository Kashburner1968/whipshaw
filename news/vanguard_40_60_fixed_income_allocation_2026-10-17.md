# VANGUARD FLIPS THE 60/40: ITS MODEL PORTFOLIO NOW FAVORS BONDS

**Whipshaw | October 17, 2026**

Vanguard has moved its time-varying portfolio allocation to **40% stocks and 60% bonds**, reversing the traditional 60% stock / 40% bond mix. The change is a concrete expression of the institutional rotation toward fixed income: less equity exposure and more weight in bonds.

Vanguard Global Head of Portfolio Construction Roger Aliaga-Díaz has described the firm's time-varying portfolios as **underweight equities**, with a **40/60 stock-bond position**. Vanguard also favors U.S. Treasuries within fixed income while taking a more cautious stance toward corporate credit and high-yield bonds.

The shift is not limited to a theoretical discussion about whether bonds might someday look attractive. Vanguard's advisor-facing guidance addresses rebalancing portfolios by selling appreciated equity holdings and purchasing bonds. That is an actionable portfolio-allocation framework.

## The allocation change

| Portfolio mix | Equities | Bonds |
|---|---:|---:|
| Conventional balanced portfolio | 60% | 40% |
| Vanguard time-varying model position | **40%** | **60%** |
| More defensive modeling scenario, previously discussed | 30% | 70% |

The **30/70** mix is a more defensive scenario discussed in asset-allocation modeling, **not** Vanguard's current universal target. Likewise, a model allocation is not proof that every Vanguard fund or client account has already traded to that weight.

## What it means for the institutional rotation

When one of the world's largest asset managers positions its time-varying model portfolio at 40/60, the shift toward fixed income has moved beyond market commentary into published portfolio construction. Together with reported bond-fund inflows and pension rebalancing, it is further evidence that fixed income is attracting real institutional demand.

The distinction that matters: **Vanguard's documented 40/60 model positioning is an allocation decision; individual client trades and total dollar flows require separate transaction or fund-flow records.**
