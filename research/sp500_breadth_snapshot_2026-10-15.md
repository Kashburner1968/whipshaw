## Why the index can diverge from constituent drawdowns

The S&P 500 is capitalization weighted: a relatively small group of large companies can have an outsized effect on index levels. The equal-weight S&P 500 proxy RSP offers a separate lens on broad participation. Drawdown-from-peak distributions, advance/decline breadth and percent-above-moving-average statistics measure different things; none should be substituted for the others.

The Motley Fool's October 15 article reports that ten leading AI stocks represented roughly 40% of S&P 500 weight, citing Bank of America research. Concentration can increase index sensitivity to the largest names, but concentration and historical drawdowns alone do not establish the timing or magnitude of future market moves.

## Reproducible follow-up measurements

To test whether index breadth is improving or deteriorating, track (1) SPY versus RSP performance on the same interval; (2) S&P 500 advance/decline counts; (3) constituents above 50-day and 200-day moving averages; (4) sector-level returns; and (5) changes in the drawdown distribution using a fixed constituent universe and as-of date. Record timestamps and data vendors.
