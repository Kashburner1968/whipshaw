# THE INDEX IS NOT THE MARKET: OCTOBER 16 BREADTH UPDATE

**Whipshaw Research Desk | October 16, 2026 | Source-attributed market analysis**

The S&P 500's capitalization-weighted headline conceals substantial dispersion across its constituents. New reporting adds a separate year-to-date measure to the previously published drawdown-from-all-time-highs snapshot.

## What the latest reports show

- **84%** of 503 S&P 500 securities were at least 10% below their *individual all-time highs*, according to The Kobeissi Letter's October 11 observation, reported by The Motley Fool October 15. This is **not** a percentage of stocks down 10% year-to-date.
- **59%** were at least 20% below their all-time highs; **41%** more than 30% below; **17%** more than 50% below, under that same historical-peak definition.
- **More than 40%** of S&P 500 stocks had negative **2026 year-to-date returns** as reported by Reuters October 12, while the capitalization-weighted S&P 500 had gained about **14%**. The median constituent was up about **4%**.
- **Fewer than half** of S&P 500 constituents traded above their 200-day moving averages in Reuters' October 12 account. This is a trend-participation statistic, distinct from both historical-peak drawdowns and YTD returns.
- On October 16, Reuters reported additional selling pressure in technology and AI-related companies, alongside investor scrutiny of debt-heavy data-center financing. These developments do not update the 84% count automatically.

## The structural issue

A cap-weighted index can advance even when the median stock participates far less. Concentrated index leadership makes the headline sensitive to a smaller set of large companies, while rising financing costs and energy prices can weigh differently on other sectors. The critical research question is whether weakness remains concentrated in particular groups or broadens into the largest index weights.

## October 16 cross-asset context

Reuters described technology and chip weakness, questions about AI infrastructure debt, and a rotation toward more defensive or cash-generating companies. Separate market reports described oil prices remaining elevated and Treasury yields fluctuating. Quotes from different reporting times must not be presented as synchronized market data.
